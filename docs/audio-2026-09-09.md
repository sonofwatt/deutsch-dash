# Audio: quick voice messages, and sound effects

Research only, asked for on 2026-09-09. Nothing here is built. It is written down
so the decision can be taken once rather than rediscovered.

Two things are being asked for and they are not the same feature. Voice is a
capture, storage and levelling problem. Sound effects are a design problem with
one hardware trap in it. They share exactly one thing: **most of the time, the
players are in the same room**, and that shapes both.

---

## Part 1: quick voice messages

### What is actually being asked for

Push-to-talk clips, not a call. Hold a button, say a sentence, everyone at the
table hears it. That is a much smaller thing than live voice, and the difference
is worth being deliberate about, because live voice is the version that needs
WebRTC, a mesh of peer connections and a signalling channel, and it would be the
heaviest thing in this app by a wide margin.

A clip is a file. This app already has somewhere to put small files.

### The shape that fits this app

The constraint that decides it: **no server of our own**, static hosting, one
Firebase Realtime Database, anonymous auth. So:

1. **Record** through the Web Audio API into `MediaRecorder`, mono, hard-capped
   at about **3 seconds**.
2. **Encode small.** Opus at 16 kbps mono is intelligible speech: about **6 kB**
   for 3 seconds, roughly 8 kB once base64 has added its third.
3. **Send it as data**, base64 in the room: `rooms/$code/voice/$pushId =
   { by, at, mime, gain, b64 }`. At 8 kB this is an ordinary RTDB write. It is
   also the only place in this app that would carry a payload worth bounding, and
   RTDB `.validate` can bound it - `newData.child('b64').val().length <= 16000` -
   which happens to close one of the gaps the 2026-09-03 audit left open.
4. **Receive** on the room listener everybody already has. Play clips whose `at`
   is newer than the moment you joined, one at a time, never your own.
5. **Sweep.** Nothing expires on its own. The sender deletes its own clip after
   playback, and the host sweeps anything older than a minute, the same shape as
   `sweepOwnRooms`.

Bandwidth: a clip costs its size times the number of listeners. Eight players,
8 kB, is 64 kB a message. A LOUD game of a hundred messages is under 7 MB against
a 10 GB monthly free tier.

**Firebase Storage was considered and is not worth it here.** It is the right home
for blobs in general, but it means another product, another set of rules, another
round trip for a download URL, and a second thing that can be misconfigured - to
move 8 kB that RTDB will carry as a string.

### Normalising it, which is the part that was actually asked for

"If someone screams into their mic it should not come out too loud." Three
separate mechanisms, and the useful answer is to use all three, because each
catches what the others miss.

**1. Ask the browser first.** `getUserMedia({ audio: { channelCount: 1,
autoGainControl: true, echoCancellation: true, noiseSuppression: true } })`. The
browser's own AGC does most of the work for free. It is also SLOW - it rides the
average level over seconds - so a sudden shout gets through it. That is what the
next one is for.

**2. Compress on the way in, before the encoder.** Build the capture graph rather
than handing the raw mic stream to `MediaRecorder`:

```
MediaStreamSource -> highpass ~100Hz -> DynamicsCompressorNode -> gain -> MediaStreamDestination
```

with the compressor around `threshold -24dB, knee 12, ratio 8, attack 3ms,
release 250ms`. A 3 ms attack catches the front of a shout, which is exactly the
transient AGC misses. Doing this BEFORE the encoder also stops the clip being
recorded clipped, which is damage no amount of playback gain can undo.

**3. Measure at record time, correct at playback.** Run an `AnalyserNode` on the
same graph, keep a running RMS, and store the gain that would bring the clip to a
target level as a number beside it. The receiver plays through
`AudioBufferSource -> gain(stored) -> limiter -> destination`. Two reasons this is
worth the extra field: nothing has to be re-encoded (re-encoding in a browser
means rendering and re-recording, which is slow and lossy), and the limiter on the
receiving side is the last line against a clip that got through the first two
anyway.

The floor matters as much as the ceiling: normalising UP a whisper recorded in a
noisy room amplifies the room. Clamp the gain to something like `x0.5 to x4` and
let a quiet clip be quiet.

### What will bite

- **Two phones at one table.** This is a game people play sitting together. A
  clip playing out of four handsets at slightly different moments is a mess, and
  the mic will hear the other phones. A "play on this device" switch, defaulting
  to on for whoever is not in the room, is a real design question rather than a
  detail.
- **iOS.** `MediaRecorder` is solid from Safari 16.4 and produces `audio/mp4`
  (AAC), not webm/opus, so both the recorder and the player have to negotiate
  through `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported`. AAC at low bitrates is bulkier than
  Opus; budget nearer 12 kB. An `AudioContext` also has to be resumed inside a
  real tap before anything will play, and the mic permission prompt has to be
  asked for somewhere calm, which means the lobby, not mid-round.
- **The silent switch.** Web Audio plays through it; `<audio>` elements do not.
  Whichever is chosen, it is the difference between a phone in a pocket staying
  quiet and not.
- **No moderation, and none available.** There is no server to filter anything.
  The mitigation that exists today is the host's Remove button.
- **Recording people who did not ask.** A push-to-talk button in a game is a
  microphone in a room. It should be obvious when it is live.

### The cheaper alternative, if this turns out to be too much

**Canned soundbites.** A dozen fixed clips - a groan, a cheer, a "come on" - shipped
with the app and triggered by writing one word to the room. No microphone, no
permission prompt, no upload, no moderation problem, no normalisation problem,
about twenty lines of code, and it covers most of what a table actually wants to
say to each other mid-round. Worth trying first, and it is not wasted work: the
playback path, the "one at a time" queue and the per-device mute switch are the
same pieces the real thing needs.

## Part 2: sound effects during play

### Events worth a sound, most valuable first

The game is real-time and silent, and the things a player currently misses are
the things that happen away from where they are looking.

**Tier 1 - the ones that carry information the eye is missing**

| event | why | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A race lost | You went for a space and somebody beat you to it. Today this is a flash you often miss because you were looking at your own hand. | The single most valuable sound in the game. |
| Somebody dashed | The round is over and it was not you. | Already has a splash; a sound gets there first. |
| Countdown 3-2-1-GO | Everyone starts at once, and the start is the whole game. | Three ticks and a chime. |
| The wood pile turning over | Your own draw pile has come round again, which is a real event in the round's rhythm. | Riffle. |
| No moves left (stuck) | The board is telling you something you may not have noticed. | Quiet nudge, not an alarm. |
| Game over | Pairs with the fireworks the winner now gets. | Fanfare for the winner, something softer for everyone else. |

**Tier 2 - feedback on your own actions**

| event | why | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card lands on a centre space | Confirms the play landed, which matters most when flicking. | The most frequent sound in the game: under 60ms, quiet, and pitch-varied per play or it becomes a machine gun. |
| Card lands on a post pile | Same, quieter and duller. | |
| A play is refused | Today this is the scowl. A sound stops you retrying the same illegal move. | Soft thud, never a buzzer. |
| Wood flip (three cards) | | Short riffle. |
| Cards dealt, round start | The hand arriving. | |

**Tier 3 - the lobby and the table around the game**

| event | note |
| --- | --- |
| A player joins the room | Soft blip. Useful while waiting for people. |
| Somebody readies up | Very quiet tick. |
| A player goes away or is removed | |
| A new centre pile is started by anyone | Risky: at eight players this fires constantly. Probably tier 3 forever. |
| All-stuck wood rotation | The whole table moves at once; worth a table-wide chime. |

### What has to be decided before any of it is built

- **Default OFF, or one device.** Four phones at one table playing the same
  dash sting a beat apart is worse than silence. This is the same trap as voice
  playback and should be settled once for both.
- **A toggle that is a device preference, not a host option**, like the wood side
  and unlike hints: whether YOUR phone makes noise is about your phone.
  `prefers-reduced-motion` says nothing about audio, so it cannot be borrowed.
- **Web Audio, not `<audio>` elements**, for anything in tier 2. Decode once at
  load into buffers and fire them from an `AudioBufferSourceNode`; an `<audio>`
  element per play adds tens of milliseconds and will not keep up with a fast
  table. One decoded sprite is the tidiest form.
- **The silent switch again**: Web Audio ignores it. A game that beeps in a
  pocket after a phone was silenced is a bug report.
- **Budget.** Twelve sounds at a few kB each is nothing next to the 140 kB gzip
  entry bundle, but they should load with the BOARD chunk, not with the entry, on
  the same reasoning that put framer-motion there.
